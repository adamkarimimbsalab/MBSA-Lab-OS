# MBSA Lab — Project Overview

## 1. Purpose

MBSA Lab is a public engineering initiative that connects model-based systems engineering (MBSE), model-based safety assessment (MBSA), verification and validation, traceability, evidence management, and practical prototyping.

Its purpose is to demonstrate how a complex engineering problem can move from intent to bounded evidence through an explicit and reviewable lifecycle.

## 2. Problem addressed

Engineering material is often divided between abstract method descriptions and isolated technical demonstrations. MBSA Lab addresses this gap by linking:

- stakeholder intent and system boundaries;
- requirements and architecture;
- safety and assurance concerns;
- implementation and configuration;
- verification and validation results;
- claims, findings, limitations, and decisions;
- reusable patterns and public communication.

The project does not assume that documentation, a model, a POC, or a successful test is sufficient on its own. Credibility depends on coherent evidence across these elements.

## 3. Scope

MBSA Lab covers:

- engineering governance;
- repository and documentation conventions;
- system context, capabilities, logical components, interfaces, and flows;
- a minimal engineering framework;
- POC lifecycle and readiness;
- traceability and evidence-package structures;
- integrated quality gates;
- pattern lifecycle and admission;
- controlled engineering communication.

The project may use automotive, embedded, software, safety, or cyber-physical examples. Examples are bounded demonstrations and do not constitute certification evidence or production approval.

## 4. Main capabilities

The target capability model includes:

1. Strategic Control — direction, prioritization, authority, and controlled baselines.
2. Governance Core — engineering rules, decisions, change control, and publication boundaries.
3. Engineering Framework — lifecycle, requirements, architecture, V&V, evidence, and traceability.
4. POC Factory — repeatable preparation, execution, assessment, and retention of proofs of concept.
5. Pattern Library — admission and controlled reuse of sufficiently demonstrated solutions.
6. Video Factory — traceable communication derived from approved engineering sources.
7. Academy — structured learning material derived from validated public content.
8. Tooling — optional automation supporting, but not replacing, engineering judgment.

Capability descriptions express intent. Their maturity is limited to what public artefacts and evidence actually demonstrate.

## 5. Engineering lifecycle

The project uses an incremental lifecycle:

1. clarify the problem, stakeholders, boundary, and assumptions;
2. define requirements and decision criteria;
3. describe architecture and interfaces;
4. plan verification, validation, traceability, and evidence;
5. implement the smallest credible demonstration;
6. assess results, findings, limitations, and residual risk;
7. decide whether to accept, iterate, defer, reject, or reuse;
8. publish only material authorized for public use.

This lifecycle can be tailored to context, but tailoring must not remove essential engineering objectives.

## 6. Evidence and maturity

MBSA Lab distinguishes:

- an intention from an implemented capability;
- an implementation from a verified result;
- a verified result from validation against stakeholder need;
- a successful POC from an admitted reusable pattern;
- technical quality from authorization to publish;
- communication material from engineering evidence.

Maturity statements therefore remain bounded and evidence-based.

## 7. Repository strategy

The public repository is the authoritative source for released public engineering material. Private administration and internal development orchestration are maintained outside the public baseline.

Changes are reviewed through a controlled Git workflow. Stable paths, explicit scope, readable diffs, and traceable decisions are preferred over unnecessary restructuring.

## 8. Documentation strategy

Numbered documents build a coherent engineering foundation. Cross-references should point to authoritative concepts rather than duplicating them. Documents use Markdown, UTF-8 without BOM, and LF line endings.

## 9. Limitations

MBSA Lab is an evolving engineering laboratory. Unless explicitly supported by released evidence, its artefacts must not be interpreted as:

- a production system;
- a safety case;
- a certification package;
- proof of tool qualification;
- authorization for operational deployment.

## 10. Long-term objective

The long-term objective is a coherent, inspectable, and reusable public engineering ecosystem in which every material claim can be traced to its context, evidence, limitations, and decision authority.
