# MBSA Lab — Vision

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the long-term direction of MBSA Lab. It establishes the intended identity and value of the initiative without claiming that all target capabilities are already implemented.

## 2. Vision statement

MBSA Lab aims to become a trusted open engineering environment where complex systems practices can be learned, demonstrated, inspected, and reused through practical examples and reproducible evidence.

The initiative connects:

- model-based systems engineering;
- model-based safety assessment;
- requirements and architecture;
- software and embedded prototyping;
- verification and validation;
- traceability and evidence management;
- engineering education and communication.

Its defining objective is not the production of isolated documents or demonstrations, but the creation of coherent evidence chains from engineering intent to justified conclusions.

## 3. Why MBSA Lab

Complex-system knowledge is frequently fragmented across methods, tools, organisations, and technical disciplines. Public examples often show an end result without exposing assumptions, failed alternatives, configuration, evidence, or limitations.

MBSA Lab seeks to make the engineering path inspectable. A reader should be able to understand:

- what problem was addressed;
- which boundary and assumptions were used;
- why an architecture or method was selected;
- how requirements and risks were treated;
- what was verified and validated;
- what the evidence does and does not prove;
- which decision authority accepted the result.

## 4. Long-term identity

MBSA Lab is intended to combine several complementary identities:

```text
Systems Engineer
    +
Software and Embedded Engineer
    +
Safety and Assurance Practitioner
    +
MBSE / MBSA Practitioner
    +
POC and Tool Builder
    +
Educator and Community Contributor
```

Engineering credibility provides the technical foundation. Education makes the reasoning accessible. POCs provide bounded evidence. Patterns support reuse. Tooling improves repeatability. Public communication makes results discoverable without replacing their engineering sources.

## 5. Target ecosystem

The target ecosystem comprises coordinated capabilities:

- strategic direction and decision authority;
- engineering governance;
- a minimal lifecycle and architecture framework;
- a repeatable POC factory;
- traceability and evidence packages;
- integrated quality gates;
- a controlled pattern library;
- a video and publication interface;
- structured educational content;
- optional supporting tools.

These capabilities may mature at different rates. A target architecture is not evidence that every component is operational.

## 6. Engineering credibility

Credibility is expected to come from the relationship between artefacts rather than their quantity. A credible demonstration connects:

1. stakeholder need and intended use;
2. system boundary and assumptions;
3. requirements and architecture;
4. implementation and configuration;
5. verification and validation procedures;
6. objective results;
7. claims, findings, limitations, and residual risks;
8. a controlled decision.

Negative results and unresolved findings remain part of the evidence. They must not be hidden to make a demonstration appear more mature.

## 7. Reuse vision

Reusable patterns should emerge only from sufficiently demonstrated work. A POC can inform a pattern, but POC success does not by itself justify pattern admission.

Reuse must preserve:

- applicability conditions;
- assumptions and dependencies;
- known limitations;
- verification evidence;
- safety and assurance implications;
- configuration and version identity.

## 8. Learning vision

MBSA Lab aims to support learners and practitioners by presenting engineering concepts at several levels:

- concise explanations of purpose and terminology;
- architecture and lifecycle views;
- bounded examples;
- inspectable evidence;
- lessons learned and reusable patterns.

Education should simplify access to engineering reasoning without simplifying away uncertainty, constraints, or risk.

## 9. Tool independence

The vision is method-led and tool-neutral. Tools may support modelling, coding, testing, traceability, evidence generation, or publication, but no specific tool is treated as a substitute for engineering judgment or decision authority.

## 10. Public value

The public baseline should allow a reader to distinguish clearly between:

- established engineering rules;
- candidate approaches;
- conceptual architecture;
- implemented demonstrations;
- verified evidence;
- admitted patterns;
- released communication material.

Private administration and internal orchestration are intentionally excluded from public deliverables.

## 11. Success horizon

The vision is progressively realized when MBSA Lab can repeatedly:

- select a relevant engineering problem;
- produce a bounded and traceable demonstration;
- evaluate it against explicit criteria;
- retain objective evidence and limitations;
- derive reusable knowledge where justified;
- communicate the result without overstating maturity.

## 12. Vision boundaries

This vision does not promise production deployment, certification, tool qualification, or universal applicability. Each future claim remains subject to the evidence and authority applicable to its context.
